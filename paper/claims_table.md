# Claims → comparison → what it shows → placement

One row per claim. **Comparison** is the plot/contrast that supports it;
**What it shows** is the conclusion that comparison licenses; **Placement**
is Main figure vs Supplement + status (have / recut / GAP). Rows tagged
**[M#]** were added in the missing-claims pass; the spike-support block (Part 2b)
is organized around the four artifact worries (W1–W4) and the discriminating
test for each. See "Considered and dropped" at the bottom.
Current status reflects the saved figure inventory after the figure audit:
some standalone panels exist but are stale relative to the argument.

The paper's spine is **descriptive — characterize a class of non-local events
that occur outside SWRs.** It has four parts, the same four as
[docs/analysis_map.md](../docs/analysis_map.md):

1. **The model:** the non-local detector, how it works, and what it captures.
2. **The events:** what they are, and how they compare with the events people
   already detect (SWRs, and the Widloski & Foster sequence detector) — **2a**
   how common they are and how they sit against SWRs, **2b** their spike
   support, **2c** their properties, **2d** the other detectors.
3. **Behavior:** how the events relate to the task, SWR against non-SWR.
4. **Other brain areas:** how they respond to the events, SWR against non-SWR.

Parts 3 and 4 compare SWR with non-SWR events only (Kay SWRs); every other
detector is compared in Part 2. The SWR contrasts (slower, more compact) are
*descriptive*, not a categorical "different kind" claim.

Claim numbers are kept from the earlier goal-based grouping, so they are not
in order within each part.
