# Claims → comparison → what it shows → placement

One row per claim. **Comparison** is the plot/contrast that supports it;
**What it shows** is the conclusion that comparison licenses; **Placement**
is Main figure vs Supplement + status (have / recut / GAP). Rows tagged
**[M#]** were added in the missing-claims pass; the spike-support block (Part 2b)
is organized around the four artifact worries (W1–W4) and the discriminating
test for each. See "Considered and dropped" at the bottom.
Current status reflects the saved figure inventory after the figure audit:
some standalone panels exist but are stale relative to the argument.

The paper's spine is **descriptive — characterize a class of non-local events
that occur outside SWRs.** It has four parts, the same four as
[docs/analysis_map.md](../docs/analysis_map.md):

1. **The model:** the non-local detector, how it works, and what it captures.
2. **The events:** what they are, and how they compare with the events people
   already detect (SWRs, and the Widloski & Foster sequence detector) — **2a**
   how common they are and how they sit against SWRs, **2b** their spike
   support, **2c** their properties, **2d** the other detectors.
3. **Behavior:** how the events relate to the task, SWR against non-SWR.
4. **Other brain areas:** how they respond to the events, SWR against non-SWR.

Parts 3 and 4 compare SWR with non-SWR events only (Kay SWRs); every other
detector is compared in Part 2. The SWR contrasts (slower, more compact) are
*descriptive*, not a categorical "different kind" claim.

Claim numbers are kept from the earlier goal-based grouping, so they are not
in order within each part.

| **Part 3 — behavior: how the events relate to the task, SWR against non-SWR** | | | | |
| | **Framing — what 3b actually shows.** **How to read every content number below.** The response is the **per-event allocation fraction** (one event, one unit of mass), fit per session with **event-clustered standard errors**, and aggregated as the animal-balanced median with animals-positive-of-5 and a session sign-rank BH-FDR *q*. Every content claim also names its **estimand**: `BASEc` rungs carry the event-length products (`log_n_nonlocal_bins_z × is_current_arm` / `× is_prev_arm`), and event length is measured **after** `is_swr`, so a `BASEc` `swr_x_…` coefficient is the length-adjusted **direct** effect while its `nt` twin on `BASEc_NT` is the **total** effect. Both are shipped and neither supersedes the other ([`content_estimator_comparison.md`](../docs/design/content_estimator_comparison.md) §8); where a conclusion moves with the likelihood, the row states all three estimators (retired count, shipped fraction, Dirichlet-multinomial). **What 3b shows.** Non-SWR content **over-represents the current arm** (near the animal but spike-supported/displaced, #19a — *not* an artifact: close events clear the reality baseline, Part 2b), and SWR content is **less** current-locked. The **last-rewarded (reward-memory) family is refused as unidentified**, so no avoidance number is quoted for it. Content is **not selective for recently rewarded arms** (#19b) — but arm *value* itself has a positive main effect in the pooled `CS5_armvalue` fit (#19g), so do not compress the two into "content ignores value". Occurrence is modelled with a smooth three-feature reward-history basis rather than per-lag dummies, and the **immediate component** (basis feature 0, kernel lag 1) is what **dissociates**. Two distinct measurements say so: the reconstructed kernel puts lag 1 at RR **1.41** (SWR-coupled non-local) against **1.09** (non-SWR) — *how large* the effect is — while a separate **paired within-session** test of the immediate basis coefficient between the two streams is positive in **69.4 % of 216 paired sessions, 5/5 animals, signed-rank p = 1.1e-11** — *how consistently* they differ (#19c). Both streams respond to immediate reward on the shared well-anchored cohort; the dissociation is one of degree. The slower components give a **mild suppression from lag 3 on** (RR ≈ 0.89–0.97) that is **shared** across all four populations. So non-SWR events are a **less consummatory/reward-coupled mode** that obeys the same slower recent-reward baseline. ## Considered and dropped (unsupported)

- **"Non-SWR events are not sub-threshold SWRs."** Contradicted by the
  ripple-power bump at non-local onset. Reframed as continuum claim #24.
- **"SWR and non-SWR events are recruited differently by behavior."** Not
  shown as a *gross* recruitment difference; the peri-switch "flat" non-SWR
  profile is a null. Superseded by the per-effect 3b decomposition (#19a–#19g),
  where non-SWR and SWR *do* differ on specific axes — immediate-reward gating
  of *occurrence* (#19c: kernel lag-1 RR **1.41** SWR-coupled vs **1.09**
  non-SWR, the paired basis coefficient positive in 69.4 % of 216 sessions) and
  avoidance of recently rewarded arms in *content* (#19f: `CS4_armreward`
  **−1.1975**, 218 sessions, **0/5** animals positive) — but share others (the
  slower recent-reward suppression of #19c, and exploration's grip on the
  current arm, where `C12_switchrate` is **+0.2582** with-SWR against
  **+0.2350** non-SWR, 5/5 each — the **same sign**, not a sign difference).
  The choice correlation differs too (#19d, `replay_frac_non_swr` **+0.34**
  against a null `replay_frac_swr`), with its scope note: that conditional-logit
  fit is in-sample and was not part of the 2026-09 refit wave.

  invalidated by task autocorrelation. The one surviving thread is the **modest,
  non-distance-controlled non-SWR `replay_frac` correlation (#19d)** — reported

  "different kind" is not claimed** — the events are *characterized* (Parts 2 and 3),
  and the SWR contrasts (slower, more compact) are descriptive, not
  categorical.
