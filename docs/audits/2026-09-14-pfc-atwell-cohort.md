# The mPFC unit GLM on the shared well-anchored stop cohort

Generated by `scripts/qa/compare_pfc_cohorts.py`. The mPFC GLM used to fit every immobile 2 ms bin in the epoch and to key its design matrices on every speed-eligible non-local event and SWR. It now fits the immobile bins that fall inside a well-anchored stop, and only the events whose onset lies inside one -- the population the hippocampal rate and content ladders are read on, selected through the same `analysis.replay.cohort_selection` functions. The regressor basis still spans +/-250 ms around each kept event; bins outside the cohort are not fit.

**The "before" columns are the current code reading the current frame, not the April run reporting its own count.** The pre-cohort bundles carry no provenance sidecar, so the before/after pair below is read from the *new* sidecars: `n_bins_immobile` is every immobile bin of the aligned decode/position frame this run saw, and `n_*_before` the events before the onset restriction. For the 27 sessions whose decode needed a trim the April run fit an untrimmed frame, so their "before" bin count is what the current code would have fit without a cohort, not what April fit. The alignment step is the identity on matched axes, so for the other 44 sessions the two coincide.

**A second, non-cohort difference between the two trees.** Every backed-up bundle carries an eleventh likelihood-ratio comparison, `SWR + non_local_no_SWR vs. non_local`; no new bundle does. That test was removed on 2026-05-06 (commit `c86a240`) because the pair is not nested unless the session has no SWR-only events. It is not one of the FDR-corrected tests and does not enter this report, but a column-wise diff of the two trees will show it.

**Cross-check on the bin mask.** The mask drops about 8% of immobile bins while the stop cohort drops 22% of stops, which is consistent: in-transit stops are short, and `docs/design/cohort_and_timing_rules.md` puts them at 7.0% of the immobility time in the corpus. The two are not the same quantity -- the PFC mask also drops immobile samples lying in no stop at all, and in `unknown` stops -- so read the agreement as a sanity check, not an identity.

**The ten-neuron change in `n_neurons_with_fit` is per-neuron convergence, not the cohort.** The two trees hold the same 71 sessions and the same units; 14 sessions differ in how many neurons produced a finite primary p-value and NaN-free modulation traces, and they differ in BOTH directions -- six sessions gain 7 neurons between them and eight lose 17 (the largest single move is wilbur20210331_08_r4, 483 to 475). A session that had lost an event class outright would move all of its neurons at once; these do not.

## Decode/position alignment

27 of 71 sidecars record the decode/position alignment step, and every one of those 27 session(s) needed a trim: 108,151 decode bins (216.3 s) dropped in total, against 0 position rows -- the decode always outlives the position frame, never the reverse. The largest single trim is 135.2 s in `wilbur20210329_16_r8`. The remaining 44 session(s) were fit before the step existed, by code whose strict 1-to-1 validator refused any mismatch at all, so their axes already agreed exactly and the step would be the identity on them.

The gated column means different things on the two sides. `immobility_avg_firing_rate` is the mean rate over the bins the bundle was fit on, so before the change it was the rate over every immobile bin and after it is the rate over the cohort's immobile bins. The new bundles also carry `immobility_avg_firing_rate_all_immobile`, the unrestricted rate, and `n_passing_all_immobile_rate` counts the neurons the *old* quantity would have admitted -- so the gate's contribution to any change in n is separable from the cohort's.
