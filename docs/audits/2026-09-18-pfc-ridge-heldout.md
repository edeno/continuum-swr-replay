# The mPFC unit GLM under a prior, scored out of fold

Generated by `scripts/qa/compare_pfc_cohorts.py`. The unit GLMs were fit by maximum likelihood and tested by a likelihood ratio against an asymptotic chi-square, with AIC selecting between models. Neither survives the fits themselves: a basis column with no spikes under its support drives its coefficient to minus infinity, and neighbouring 2 ms bins are not independent, so an in-sample deviance drop is worth much less than its nominal degrees of freedom. The models now carry a Gaussian prior on the event-basis coefficients -- loose enough that the spikes shape any modulation they can support -- and are compared by how well they predict spikes they were not fit on, in folds of whole immobility runs, against a null built by redrawing the duration-matched non-event control.

## What the prior did to the fits

Each bundle carries, beside every peri-event curve, the peak of the penalised curve, the peak the same coefficient block reaches **unpenalised**, and that peak's own standard error -- the standard error of the kernel at its peak lag, off-diagonal terms included. So the prior's effect is read inside one fit of one design, not by differencing two trees that were fit on different bases.

`prior_binds` counts units whose penalised peak differs from the maximum-likelihood one by more than that error. A unit whose unpenalised fit ran away has no peak to difference against: it is reported on its own row (`separated`), which is where a prior is supposed to do the work, and is left out of the binding count.

### The prior the corpus's own spread would have chosen

The unpenalised peaks scatter for two reasons: units really do modulate by different amounts, and each peak is estimated with error. Deconvolving the second -- `sqrt(var(peak_mle) - mean(se_mle**2))` over the well-posed units -- leaves the first, which is a prior standard deviation for the kernel at one lag. Divided by what one unit of sigma buys at one lag it is an empirical-Bayes estimate of the per-coefficient sigma, to be read against the value the bundles were actually fit at (`penalty_sigma_log_rate` = 3.0, implied per-lag sd 2.066, both from the sidecar). A chosen sigma well above the implied one is a deliberately loose prior; one below it would be shaping modulations the spikes can support.

## What the two inferences call

Before, BH-corrected likelihood-ratio p-values at q < 0.05; after, the descriptive fraction of units with raw empirical p < 0.05 from the control-draw null. The latter is not population inference: that requires aggregation through session and animal. The two are not comparable test by test -- they test different things against different references -- so read them as two accounts of the same population, not as a movement. `n_tested` is the units with a finite value for that test: a unit whose session had no events of a class is untested for it, not a negative.

## How the drawn windows are spaced, beside the real ones

Gaps are between consecutive onsets of one window set, in milliseconds, measured inside one window set; `frac_closer_than_source_duration` is the share of consecutive onsets whose gap is under the earlier window's own duration. Real events cannot overlap, so their fraction is zero by construction. Control onsets are drawn distinct but their duration-matching windows may overlap, which is what makes an exact count-matched draw possible in a dense bout; the price is a drawn regressor whose onsets can bunch where a real one's never do. A null row is the median over the first 20 recorded draws of that draw's own statistic (the last column is the mean over draws), each draw rebuilt from its own recorded seed; the draws are never pooled onto one time axis, which would divide every gap by the number of draws.

## How much of the engagement null is a copy of its baseline

The engagement statistic's null draws are independent of the shipped matched control they are entered beside, so a draw may place a window where that control already sits -- something two real event classes never do, because each class's windows avoid the others'. The cost is measured rather than removed: blocking it would draw the null by different rules from the control it stands in for. The larger this fraction, the more of the null's added regressor is a copy of the baseline it is entered beside, and the more anti-conservative the engagement p-value.

## Units called with no out-of-fold gain

The empirical p-value is one-sided against the control-draw null, so a unit can sit below 0.05 while its own observed held-out improvement over the constant model is zero or negative: the draws simply did worse. Those units are evidence that the class beats a content-free regressor of the same size, not that the class predicts held-out spikes better than a constant rate. ## Events the matched test could not use

An event with no legal non-event window inside its own fitted bout cannot enter a matched randomisation test, so one fixed subset -- selected from timing and duration alone, never from spikes -- is used on both the observed and the null side. The empirical p-value is therefore a statement about that subset, not about every event of the class; the all-event held-out difference remains separately in the bundle.
